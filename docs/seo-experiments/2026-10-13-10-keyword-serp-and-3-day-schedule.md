## Keyword-by-Keyword Top 7 Google SERP Analysis & Synthesis

### 1. `how long to rest between sets`
* **Why people search it:** Lifters want a clear rule for how long to rest on heavy squats/bench versus curls/lateral raises, and how to finish on time when their workout is capped at 40 minutes.
* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**
  1. **Healthline** — Ranks for clear goal-based buckets (3–5 min for strength, 60–90s or 1–3 min for hypertrophy, 30–60s for muscular endurance) and medical review citations.
  2. **Stronger By Science** — Ranks for deep synthesis of Schoenfeld et al. (2016) showing 2–3+ minutes outperforms 60-second rests for hypertrophy by preserving total volume load.
  3. **PubMed / NIH (Schoenfeld et al. 2016; Grgic et al. 2017)** — Ranks as the primary scientific authority on inter-set rest intervals and phosphocreatine recovery.
  4. **Men's Health** — Ranks for scannable exercise-tier rules (compound vs. isolation) and practical gym-floor cues.
  5. **NASM (National Academy of Sports Medicine)** — Ranks for ATP-PC replenishment timeline tables (85–95% ATP recovery in 2–3 minutes).
  6. **GymBeam / Hevy Blog** — Ranks for practical workout-timer comparisons and pairing antagonist supersets to save time.
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` / `r/weightroom`)** — Ranks because lifters ask what to do when resting 3 minutes makes a workout take 90 minutes.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Opens with the exact rest-by-lift table, cites Schoenfeld (2016) and ATP-PC replenishment timelines, adds the **3-Check Readiness Test** (breathing, local burn, mental focus), explains **Antagonist Paired Sets (APS)** for 40-minute sessions, and embeds an interactive **Rest & Session-Time Calculator**.

### 2. `4 day upper lower workout split`
* **Why people search it:** Lifters moving past 3-day full-body training want a balanced Monday/Tuesday/Thursday/Friday template that hits every muscle twice weekly without living in the gym.
* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**
  1. **Muscle & Strength** — Ranks for immediate, printable 4-day tables with sets and rep ranges right below the intro.
  2. **Built With Science (Jeremy Ethier)** — Ranks for separating Upper A / Lower A (horizontal/quad focus) from Upper B / Lower B (vertical/hinge focus) to prevent joint overuse.
  3. **BarBend** — Ranks for clear progression rules and customization options for strength vs. hypertrophy.
  4. **AWorkoutRoutine** — Ranks for stripped-down, no-fluff exercise selection (5–6 movements per session, 10–16 weekly sets per muscle).
  5. **LoadMuscle / MacrosInc** — Ranks for weekly scheduling calendars (Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri vs. Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat) and recovery guidelines.
  6. **Fitbod Blog** — Ranks for showing how to swap barbell lifts for dumbbells or machines inside an upper/lower structure.
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` PHUL / Lyle McDonald Generic Bulking Routine)** — Ranks because lifters ask what to do when they miss Friday's Lower B workout.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Provides pre-rendered Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, and Lower B tables with barbell + dumbbell equivalents, explains the quad/hinge and horizontal/vertical rotation, and adds the missing gap: an interactive **4-Day Upper/Lower Schedule & Missed-Day Compression Builder**.

### 4. `reps in reserve rir chart`
* **Why people search it:** Lifters see "1–2 RIR" or "RPE 8" in programs and want a simple chart translating RIR to RPE, percentage of 1RM, and how to pick weights when tired.
* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**
  1. **NASM Blog** — Ranks for clear definition of technical failure vs. absolute failure and why 1–3 RIR maximizes hypertrophy with lower injury risk.
  2. **MASS Research Review / Helms et al. (2016)** — Ranks as the original scientific source for the RIR-based RPE scale in resistance training.
  3. **GymAware** — Ranks for connecting RIR to bar speed slowdown and autoregulation on low-sleep days.
  4. **Alpha Progression / RP Strength** — Ranks for explaining how RIR changes from Week 1 (3 RIR) to Week 4 (0–1 RIR) across a mesocycle.
  5. **RPE Training / Barbell Medicine** — Ranks for the complete side-by-side RPE 6–10 to 0–4 RIR conversion table.
  6. **Stronger By Science** — Ranks for addressing the biggest flaw of RIR: beginners underestimating how many reps they have left on 12+ rep sets.
  7. **PubMed (Refalo et al. 2023; Zourdos et al. 2016)** — Ranks for peer-reviewed evidence comparing proximity-to-failure zones for muscle growth vs. neuromuscular fatigue.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Presents the complete **RIR-to-RPE Conversion Table**, explains when to use 2–3 RIR (barbell squats/deadlifts) vs. 0–1 RIR (cables/curls/lateral raises), and embeds an interactive **RIR Autoregulation & Next-Set Weight Adjuster**.

  1. **Healthline** — Ranks for clear side-by-side joint-action diagrams (floor dead stop with knee extension vs. top-down hip hinge with fixed soft knees).
  2. **REP Fitness** — Ranks for explaining lengthened-position hamstring tension on RDLs vs. quad drive off the floor on conventional deadlifts.
  3. **Garage Gym Reviews** — Ranks for barbell vs. dumbbell execution cues and common lower-back rounding fixes.
  4. **Sweat / Stronger Mobile App** — Ranks for programming advice (when to put conventional deadlifts first vs. RDLs after squats).

  6. **PubMed (Martín-Fuentes et al. 2020; McAllister et al. 2014)** — Ranks for EMG comparisons of posterior-chain activation across deadlift variations.
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` / `r/weightroom`)** — Ranks because lifters ask "How much should I RDL compared to my conventional deadlift?" (typically 65%–80%).

  2. **PureGym** — Ranks for translating degrees into practical bench notches ("1 to 2 clicks up from flat").
  3. **Athlean-X** — Ranks for scapular retraction and arm-path cues (30°–45° elbow tuck in the scapular plane instead of flaring at 90°).
  4. **Bells of Steel** — Ranks for explaining why 15° ("low incline") is a secret weapon for lifters with shoulder impingement.
  5. **Dumbbells Direct / Speediance** — Ranks for comparing flat-to-incline dumbbell weight ratios.
  6. **Men's Health** — Ranks for form checklists (keeping glutes and upper back planted rather than arching into a flat press).
  7. **Reddit (`r/naturalbodybuilding` / `r/Fitness`)** — Ranks for real-world bench-notch measurements (how to tell 30° from 45° using seat-to-backrest height).
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Summarizes the Rodríguez-Ridao et al. (2020) EMG findings across 0°/15°/30°/45°/60°, gives the **seat-to-top-of-pad measurement rule** to find 30° on any unnumbered gym bench, and embeds an interactive **Incline Angle & Dumbbell Load Converter**.

### 8. `how much protein per meal to build muscle`
* **Why people search it:** Lifters want to know if eating 50g–60g of protein in one meal is "wasted" (the old 30g myth) and how to split their daily protein target across 3, 4, or 5 meals.
* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**
  1. **PubMed / Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition (Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018)** — Ranks as the definitive review establishing `0.40 to 0.55 g/kg/meal` across at least 4 meals.
  2. **Cell Reports Medicine (Trommelen et al. 2023)** — Ranks for the landmark tracer study showing 100g of protein in a single meal sustains muscle protein synthesis for 12+ hours rather than being "wasted."
  3. **Examine.com** — Ranks for separating intestinal absorption (virtually 100%) from acute 4-hour muscle protein synthesis rates.
  4. **Healthline / Mayo Clinic** — Ranks for practical food-equivalent examples (what 35g–45g of protein looks like on a plate).
  5. **MacroFactor / Stronger By Science** — Ranks for showing that total daily protein (`1.6–2.2 g/kg/day`) accounts for the vast majority of results, with meal distribution as a secondary optimization.
  6. **MyProteinCalc / Fit-Calc** — Ranks for quick body-weight-to-per-meal lookup tables.
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` / `r/gainit`)** — Ranks because busy workers who only eat 2 or 3 meals a day want to know if they can still build muscle.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Cites both Schoenfeld & Aragon (2018) and Trommelen et al. (2023), explains digestion vs. acute MPS without hype, gives real-food plate examples, and embeds an interactive **Per-Meal Protein Distribution Calculator** for 2, 3, 4, or 5 meals per day.

* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**

  2. **Bony to Beastly** — Ranks for explaining why crash dieting without lifting leaves beginners "skinny-fat at a lower weight" and why progressive resistance training is mandatory.
  3. **PubMed (Barakat et al. 2020, *Strength and Conditioning Journal*)** — Ranks for peer-reviewed evidence that untrained lifters and individuals with higher body fat can simultaneously build muscle and lose fat (body recomposition).
  4. **Built With Science / Jeff Nippard** — Ranks for pairing high protein (`1.6–2.2 g/kg`) with a 3-day full-body compound lifting plan and daily step targets (`7,000–9,000 steps`).
  5. **Healthline** — Ranks for health context on visceral adipose tissue, sleep, and resistance exercise.
  6. **IronLair / LoadMuscle** — Ranks for concrete 12-week expectations (why waist circumference and gym strength change before scale weight moves).
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` Body Recomposition Wiki)** — Ranks because beginners want a simple numeric rule to decide between maintenance calories and a slight deficit.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Gives the **3-Scenario Calorie Decision Rule** (Maintenance vs. 10–15% Deficit vs. 5–10% Surplus), a 3-day full-body foundation template, non-scale progress markers (waist measurement + 8-rep compound strength), and an interactive **Recomposition Calorie, Protein & Split Planner**.

### 10. `returning to gym after missing a week` (or 2 weeks)
* **Why people search it:** Lifters who missed 7 to 14 days due to travel, work crunch, or a mild cold feel guilty or rusty and want to know how much weight to take off the bar so they don't get crippled by soreness (DOMS).
* **Top 7 Ranking Websites & Why They Rank High:**
  1. **PubMed (Ogasawara et al. 2013; Hwang et al. 2017)** — Ranks for detraining/retraining studies showing 1 to 3 weeks off causes negligible muscle loss and full catch-up once training resumes.
  2. **Stronger By Science** — Ranks for distinguishing temporary glycogen/water loss and neural rustiness from true muscle atrophy.
  3. **Physio-Central / BStrong** — Ranks for explaining the "repeated bout effect" fade—why eccentric lowering causes extra DOMS after 10–14 days off, and why cutting volume (sets) matters more than just cutting weight.
  4. **GymNotePlus / DocLyssFitness** — Ranks for concrete percentage rules (80–85% of pre-break working load after 1 week off; 70–80% and minus 1–2 sets after 2 weeks off).
  5. **BarBend** — Ranks for advising lifters to return to their existing program rather than program-hopping out of guilt.
  6. **Mayo Clinic / ACSM** — Ranks for the "neck rule" and medical recovery guidance when returning after illness.
  7. **Reddit (`r/Fitness` comeback threads)** — Ranks because lifters want a simple 2-week ramp-back schedule to get back to 100% of their previous numbers.
* **How Our Blog Combines All 7:** Cites Ogasawara et al. (2013) on short breaks, explains why cutting **1 set per exercise and leaving 2–3 RIR** prevents crippling DOMS, provides a **Days-Off Ramp-Back Table (3–5 days, 7–10 days, 14–21 days, 4+ weeks)**, and embeds an interactive **Post-Break Comeback Weight & Set Calculator**.
