3-Day Dumbbell Workout Plan: Full-Body A / B / C Routine

Train your entire body across three non-consecutive days using only dumbbells. Each session rotates primary movement patterns, includes no-bench floor alternatives, and gives you a clear compression rule if a busy week costs you a workout.

By IZEM Editorial · Accountable publisher: Mohammed Jebbari · Updated October 13, 2026

Workout A (Mon) Workout B (Wed) Workout C (Fri) Load & time planner 4-week progression Missed-workout rule Sources

How this program works: Schedule Workout A on Monday, Workout B on Wednesday, and Workout C on Friday (or any three days with at least one rest day between sessions). Every workout trains legs, push, pull, and accessories in 35 to 50 minutes so each muscle group receives stimulus three times per week [1].

General strength training guidance for healthy adults. Warm up before lifting, keep every repetition under control, and stop if an exercise causes sharp joint or back pain. Consult a physician or physical therapist for individualized medical or injury guidance.

Workout A (Day 1): Knee-Dominant Squat & Horizontal Push/Pull

Workout A opens the week with bilateral squat mechanics, horizontal pressing, and upper-back rowing. Leave 1 to 2 Reps in Reserve (RIR) on compound lifts so your posture stays stable across all three sets.

Order & Exercise Primary Target Sets × Reps Rest No-Bench / Floor Alternative
1. Dumbbell Goblet Squat Quads, glutes, core 3 × 8 to 12 90 sec Same movement (hold one heavy dumbbell vertically at chest height)
2. Dumbbell Bench Press Chest, front delts, triceps 3 × 8 to 12 90 sec Dumbbell Floor Press (pause triceps gently on floor for 1 second)
3. One-Arm Dumbbell Row Lats, mid-back, biceps 3 × 8 to 12 / side 60-90 sec Kickstand Bent-Over Dumbbell Row (brace free forearm on front thigh)
4. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL) Hamstrings, glutes 3 × 10 to 12 90 sec B-Stance (Kickstand) Dumbbell RDL if your heaviest pair feels light
5. Standing Dumbbell Lateral Raise Lateral deltoids 2-3 × 12 to 15 60 sec Same movement (slight forward lean in the scapular plane)
6. Supinated Dumbbell Bicep Curl Biceps, forearms 2 × 10 to 12 60 sec Same movement Standing or Kneeling

Workout B (Day 2): Hip Hinge, Overhead Press & Posterior Chain

After a rest day, Workout B shifts primary fatigue to the posterior chain and vertical pressing. If your lower back feels fatigued from Monday, use the chest-supported row option or brace one hand on a sturdy surface.

Order & Exercise Primary Target Sets × Reps Rest No-Bench / Floor Alternative
1. Dumbbell Deadlift or Sumo Squat Glutes, hamstrings, quads 3 × 8 to 10 90 sec Elevating the bells on two thick books or plates reduces shin reach
2. Seated or Standing Overhead Press Front/side delts, triceps 3 × 8 to 12 90 sec Standing Neutral-Grip Dumbbell Shoulder Press (ribs stacked over pelvis)
3. Dumbbell Reverse Lunge Quads, glutes, hip stability 3 × 8 to 10 / leg 90 sec Stationary Split Squat (keep both feet planted between reps)
4. Chest-Supported Incline DB Row Upper back, rear delts, lats 3 × 10 to 12 75 sec Two-Dumbbell Hinge Row with 2-second squeeze at top
5. Dumbbell Glute Bridge / Hip Thrust Glutes, hamstrings 3 × 12 to 15 60 sec Single-Leg Floor Dumbbell Glute Bridge (2-second top hold)
6. Overhead DB Triceps Extension Triceps long head 2 × 10 to 15 60 sec Floor Lying Dumbbell Triceps Extension (Skull Crusher)

Workout C (Day 3): Unilateral Strength & Upper-Body Hypertrophy

Workout C closes the week with single-leg work, upper-chest pressing, and grip/core stabilization. For detailed foot-placement cues on the first movement, open our Bulgarian Split Squat setup guide.

Order & Exercise Primary Target Sets × Reps Rest No-Bench / Floor Alternative
1. Dumbbell Bulgarian Split Squat Quads, glutes, adductors 3 × 8 to 10 / leg 90 sec Front-Foot-Elevated Split Squat or Slow-Eccentric Reverse Lunge
2. Incline Dumbbell Press Upper chest, front delts 3 × 10 to 12 90 sec Bridge Floor Press or Feet-Elevated Push-Up + Dumbbell Fly
3. Dumbbell Pullover or Tripod Row Lats, serratus, upper back 3 × 10 to 12 75 sec Floor Dumbbell Pullover (elbows slightly bent, tap bells lightly to floor)
4. Single-Leg or Stiff-Leg DB RDL Hamstrings, glute medius 3 × 10 to 12 75 sec Use one hand lightly against a wall for balance on single-leg reps
5. Dumbbell Hammer Curl Brachialis, brachioradialis 2 × 10 to 14 60 sec Cross-Body or Slow-Lowering Hammer Curl
6. Dumbbell Farmer's Carry or Suitcase Hold Forearms, traps, lateral core 3 × 35 to 45 sec 60 sec Single-Arm Suitcase March in Place if room length is short

4-week double-progression rule for dumbbells

Because fixed dumbbells usually jump by 5 lb (or 2 to 2.5 kg) per hand, adding weight every single week quickly ruins form. Controlled studies show that building reps with a lighter or moderate load produces comparable muscle growth provided sets finish near fatigue [2]. Use this four-step progression ladder before reaching for the next pair:

  1. Week 1 (Base reps): Choose a weight where you hit the bottom of the rep range (for example, 3 sets of 8 reps) with 2 Reps in Reserve.
  2. Week 2 (Add repetitions): Keep the same dumbbells and aim for 9 to 10 reps per set.
  3. Week 3 (Own the top of the range): Hit 12 controlled reps across all 3 working sets without cutting depth or bouncing at the bottom.
  4. Week 4 (Move up 1 dumbbell pair): Increase the weight and reset your target to 8 reps per set. If your home set jumps by 10 lb per hand, extend the rep ceiling to 15 reps or add a 2-second bottom pause before moving up.

How to adjust when you miss Wednesday or Friday

If a busy week knocks out one of your three workouts, avoid restarting Workout A next week while leaving Workout C untouched for two weeks. Instead, use the 45-Minute B+C Compression Session on your final training day of the week:

  • 1. Dumbbell Bulgarian Split Squat or Reverse Lunge: 3 × 8 to 10 per leg (covers unilateral knee-dominant work from B and C).
  • 2. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift: 3 × 8 to 12 (covers hip-hinge stimulus from B and C).
  • 3. Seated or Standing Dumbbell Overhead Press: 3 × 8 to 12 (covers vertical pressing from B).
  • 4. Chest-Supported or Single-Arm Dumbbell Row: 3 × 10 to 12 (covers horizontal pulling from B and C).
  • 5. Superset: Hammer Curl + Overhead Triceps Extension: 2 × 10 to 12 each with 45 seconds rest.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build muscle with a 3-day dumbbell workout plan?

Yes. Resistance training meta-analyses show that muscle hypertrophy is similar across moderate and lighter loads when sets are performed with high effort (1 to 2 RIR) and weekly set volume reaches roughly 10 or more sets per major muscle group [1] [2].

Do I need a workout bench for this 3-day dumbbell routine?

No. A bench increases pressing range of motion and chest-supported row stability, but every exercise in Workout A, B, and C includes a floor-only equivalent such as the dumbbell floor press, kickstand row, and reverse lunge.

How do I progress when my dumbbells only increase by 5 lb (2.5 kg) per hand?

Use a wider repetition bracket (for example, 8 to 14 reps on presses and rows, or 12 to 18 reps on lateral raises), slow the lowering phase to three seconds, or add a one-second pause at the bottom before jumping to the next dumbbell pair [3].
