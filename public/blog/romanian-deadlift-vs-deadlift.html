Exercise Comparison · Posterior Chain

Use conventional deadlifts for floor pulling strength and RDLs for hamstring growth.

Although both lifts share a name, they start in opposite positions and load your legs differently. A conventional deadlift starts from a dead stop on the floor using both quads and hips; a Romanian deadlift starts standing and keeps constant eccentric tension on the hamstrings and glutes.

Core mechanical difference: The conventional deadlift begins on the floor with bent knees and uses leg drive plus hip extension to break inertia. The Romanian deadlift (RDL) begins from the top lockout with a slight, fixed 15° to 20° knee bend, lowering only until backward hip travel ends (usually mid-shin) so the hamstrings stay under continuous stretched tension [1] [2].

Romanian deadlift vs. conventional deadlift comparison table

Electromyography (EMG) and biomechanical analyses show that removing the floor reset and restricting knee flexion shifts workload away from the quadriceps and concentrates mechanical tension in the hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, and semimembranosus) and gluteus maximus [1] [2].

Why the RDL builds hamstrings faster per unit of lower-back fatigue

Because the hamstrings cross both the hip and knee joints, bending your knees deeply at the bottom of a conventional deadlift slackens the lower end of the hamstrings while the hips flex. In an RDL, keeping your knees nearly fixed while pushing your hips straight back Toward the wall behind you stretches the hamstrings across a long muscle length under load [2].

Controlled hypertrophy studies show that training muscles at longer muscle lengths (such as the stretched bottom of an RDL or seated leg curl) stimulates strong regional muscle growth [3]. Better yet, because an RDL uses roughly 70% of your floor deadlift weight for sets of 8 to 10 reps, it places less peak compressive shear on the lumbar spine.

How to program both lifts in the same week

  • On a 4-day Upper/Lower split: Place Barbell Squats + Dumbbell or Barbell RDLs (3 × 8 to 10) on Tuesday (Lower A), and place Conventional or Trap-Bar Deadlifts (3 × 5) on Friday (Lower B) when your lower back has had 72 hours of recovery.
  • The mid-shin stop rule on RDLs: Never judge an RDL by whether the plates touch the floor. Lower the bar only as far as your hips can travel backward with a neutral spine. The instant your hips stop moving back, any extra inch toward the floor comes from rounding your lower back.

Frequently asked questions

Can I replace conventional deadlifts with Romanian deadlifts permanently?

Unless you compete in powerlifting or strongman, yes. You do not have to pull from the floor to build a strong back, glutes, and hamstrings. Pairing squats or leg presses with RDLs covers both knee extension and hip extension thoroughly.

How much weight should I use on Dumbbell RDLs compared to Barbell RDLs?

Because each hand holds an independent dumbbell and grip fatigue accumulates over 8 to 12 continuous reps, most lifters start with roughly 40% to 45% of their Barbell RDL total weight in each hand (80% to 90% combined).

Are lifting straps okay to use on Romanian deadlifts?

Yes. On sets of 8 to 12 continuous RDLs where the bar never rests on the floor, forearm grip often gives out long before your hamstrings and glutes reach 1 to 2 RIR. Using straps on your heaviest working sets prevents grip from bottlenecking leg growth.
