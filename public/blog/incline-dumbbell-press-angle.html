Best Incline Dumbbell Press Angle: Why 15° to 30° Beats 45° for Upper Chest

Walk into any gym and you will see lifters pressing on the third or fourth bench notch, wondering why their front delts burn while their upper chest stays flat. Surface EMG data and fiber mechanics point to a much shallower angle.

What Happens Between 0° and 60°: The EMG Breakdown

In 2020, Rodríguez-Ridao and colleagues measured surface electromyography (EMG) of the upper chest (clavicular head), middle/lower chest (sternal head), anterior deltoid, and triceps brachii across five bench angles: 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, and 60° (Rodríguez-Ridao et al., 2020). A companion resistance-trained trial by Lauver et al. reported the same pattern (Lauver et al., 2016).

As the backrest tilts upward, two things happen simultaneously: the humerus path aligns better with the clavicular fibers between 15° and 30°, and shoulder flexion demand climbs steadily. Past 30°, the anterior deltoid takes over so aggressively that upper-chest recruitment actually drops below its 30° peak.

How to Find 15° and 30° on an Unnumbered Gym Bench

Many commercial benches do not stamp degree markings onto the adjustment ladder. You do not need a protractor—use these three quick checks:

  1. The 1-to-2 click rule: Lay the bench completely flat (0°). Lift the backrest until it drops into the very first slot. On 90% of commercial benches (Rogue, Life Fitness, Hammer Strength, Rep), Notch 1 is 15° and Notch 2 is 30°.
  2. The halfway-to-45° visual check: Everyone can picture a 90° upright seat and a 45° diagonal halfway mark. A 30° incline sits noticeably below that 45° diagonal—roughly one-third of the way from flat to upright.
  3. The iPhone Measure app check: Open the built-in Level tool in your phone's Measure app and lay the long edge of your phone against the backrest pad. It takes five seconds once and tells you the exact angle of Notch 1 and Notch 2 on your gym's benches.

Why a Huge Arch Turns a 30° Incline Back Into a Flat Press

Selecting the 30° notch only works if your ribcage stays near 30°. When you try to move too much weight and bridge your thoracic spine hard off the pad, your sternum tilts back toward horizontal. You have effectively turned a 30° incline press back into a decline or flat press with a shorter range of motion.

  • Keep your upper back and glutes glued to the pad: Retract and slightly depress your shoulder blades for stability, but avoid an exaggerated powerlifting ribcage flare.
  • Raise the seat pad 1 notch: Tilting the bottom seat pad up by 10°–15° stops your hips from sliding down the bench so you don't have to arch excessively to stay anchored.
  • Tuck your elbows 45° to 60°: Flaring your elbows out to 90° crowds the subacromial space and stresses the anterior shoulder capsule. Tucking your elbows slightly in front of the shoulder line matches the upward-and-inward orientation of the clavicular pec fibers (Schoenfeld et al., 2021).
  • Lower the dumbbells to the outer clavicle: Aim the inner plates of the dumbbells toward your upper chest/collarbone line, pause for half a second in the stretch, and press up without clanking the dumbbells together at the top.

Should You Use 15°, 30°, or Both in Your Program?

You do not have to pick one angle forever. In fact, rotating or pairing 15° and 30° across an upper/lower split or push/pull/legs routine gives you complete chest coverage:

  • Use 15° (Notch 1) as your primary heavy press: Because 15° recruits both the sternal and clavicular heads heavily while allowing 90%–95% of your flat dumbbell load, it can replace flat bench entirely if flat pressing irritates your shoulders.
  • Use 30° (Notch 2) as your dedicated upper-chest builder: When you already perform a flat barbell bench press (calculated via our 1RM calculator), follow it with 3 sets of 8–12 reps on the 30° incline dumbbell press leaving 1–2 reps in reserve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 30° or 45° better for the incline dumbbell press?

30° is better for targeting the upper chest. Surface EMG studies show clavicular pectoralis major activation peaks at 30°, whereas 45° significantly increases anterior deltoid involvement and reduces chest activation.

How much less weight do you lift on incline dumbbell press compared to flat?

On a 15° low incline, most lifters handle 90% to 95% of their flat dumbbell press weight for the same reps. On a 30° incline, expect to use roughly 82% to 88% of your flat dumbbell weight when fresh, or 75% to 80% if performed second after flat bench.

Should dumbbells touch at the top of an incline press?

No. Clanking the dumbbells together at the top often causes you to protract your shoulder blades and lose upper-back tension, and gravity pulls straight down at lockout where there is zero horizontal adduction resistance.
