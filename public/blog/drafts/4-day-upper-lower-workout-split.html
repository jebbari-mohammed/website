Workout Split Template · 4 Days / Week

Train upper body on Monday and Thursday, lower body on Tuesday and Friday.

A 4-day upper/lower split trains every major muscle group twice a week while leaving Wednesday and the entire weekend open for recovery. Rotating A and B sessions prevents joint overuse and keeps workouts under 50 minutes.

By IZEM Editorial · Accountable publisher: Mohammed Jebbari · 2026-10-19

Why 4-day upper/lower works: Hitting each muscle group twice per week produces stronger hypertrophy outcomes than once-weekly body-part splits at equal volume [1]. Separating upper and lower sessions lets you train two days in a row (Monday/Tuesday and Thursday/Friday) without fatigued synergist muscles ruining Tuesday's lifts.

Why rotate A and B days instead of repeating the same workout?

Repeating heavy flat barbell bench presses and floor deadlifts twice every week wears down your shoulders and lower back. The cleanest 4-day upper/lower routines bias Upper A and Lower A toward horizontal pressing and knee-dominant squatting, then bias Upper B and Lower B toward incline/vertical pressing and hip-hinge work [2].

4-Day Upper/Lower Plan

What to do when you miss Friday's Lower B workout

Life rarely respects a rigid 4-day calendar every single week. If a travel day or late meeting wipes out one of your back-half sessions, do not leave your lower body trained only once that week. Convert your final session of the week into a 45-Minute Upper/Lower Hybrid:

  • 1. Primary Hinge (from Lower B): Romanian Deadlift or Trap-Bar Deadlift — 3 × 6 to 8
  • 2. Primary Incline Push (from Upper B): 30° Incline Dumbbell Press — 3 × 8 to 10
  • 3. Primary Vertical Pull (from Upper B): Lat Pulldown or Chin-Up — 3 × 8 to 10
  • 4. Unilateral Leg (from Lower B): Walking Lunge or Leg Press — 3 × 10 to 12
  • 5. Arm/Shoulder Pair: Lateral Raise + Triceps Extension — 2 × 12 to 15

Frequently asked questions

Is a 4-day upper/lower split better than Push/Pull/Legs?

If you can train 4 days per week, an upper/lower split hits every muscle group twice every 7 days. A classic Push/Pull/Legs split requires 6 days per week to achieve that same twice-weekly muscle frequency.

Why does Lower A come right after Upper A on consecutive days?

Because Monday trains chest, back, shoulders, and arms while Tuesday trains quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves. Only grip and spinal stabilizers overlap, which is why Tuesday starts with squats and places heavy floor deadlifts on Friday after Wednesday's rest day.

How many total sets per muscle group does this 4-day split provide?

Across Monday through Friday, chest, back, quads, and hamstrings each receive 10 to 14 hard working sets—right inside the 10 to 20 weekly set window linked to hypertrophy in resistance-training meta-analyses.
